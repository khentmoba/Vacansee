ABSTRACT
Finding available boarding house accommodations remains inefficient due to the reliance on manual searching methods and outdated vacancy information. Tenants, particularly students, often depend on walking around communities, personal referrals, or social media platforms, while boarding house owners post availability updates through signboards or online posts that are rarely maintained in real time. This situation leads to miscommunication, repeated inquiries, and wasted time for both parties. This study proposes the development of a Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website designed to centralize and update boarding house vacancy information in real time. The system enables boarding house owners to manage room availability efficiently while allowing tenants to view accurate and accessible vacancy information online. The proposed system aims to improve communication, reduce unnecessary inquiries, and provide a more organized approach to managing boarding house vacancies, thereby enhancing the overall efficiency of the boarding house search process.

Chapter 1

THE PROBLEM AND ITS SCOPE


1.1 Introduction
Finding available boarding house accommodations remains a challenge due to the continued reliance on manual and unstructured methods of tracking room vacancies. Tenants, particularly students seeking affordable housing, often depend on physically visiting locations, asking acquaintances, or browsing social media platforms to locate available rooms. Meanwhile, boarding house owners typically post vacancy updates on signboards or online platforms without consistent maintenance, resulting in outdated or inaccurate information.

This situation leads to inefficiencies such as repeated inquiries, miscommunication, and wasted time for both tenants and boarding house owners. The lack of a centralized and reliable system for managing boarding house vacancy information highlights the need for a more organized approach. With the increasing use of web-based systems for information access and management, developing a centralized platform to track and update boarding house vacancies presents a practical solution.

This study proposes the development of a Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website that enables boarding house owners to update room availability in real time and allows tenants to view accurate vacancy information online. The proposed system aims to improve accessibility, communication, and efficiency in the boarding house search and management process.


1.2 Statement of the Problem
The process of tracking and searching for available boarding house rooms is currently inefficient and unreliable due to the absence of a centralized system. This study seeks to address the following problems:
General Problem:
How can the process of tracking and accessing boarding house vacancy information be improved to ensure efficiency, accuracy, and convenience for both tenants and boarding house owners?

Specific Problems:
How does the reliance on manual methods affect the efficiency of tenants searching for available boarding house rooms?
How does outdated vacancy information contribute to miscommunication and repeated inquiries between tenants and boarding house owners?
What difficulties do boarding house owners encounter in managing and updating room availability information?
How can a web-based system improve the accessibility and reliability of boarding house vacancy information?

1.3 Statement of Objectives 
General Objective
The general objective of this study is to design and develop a web-based Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website that improves the management and accessibility of boarding house vacancy information.Specifically, this study aims to:
Analyze the current methods used by tenants and boarding house owners in searching for and updating boarding house vacancy information.
Design a centralized system that allows boarding house owners to update room availability in real time.
Develop a platform that enables tenants to view accurate and up-to-date boarding house vacancy information online.
Evaluate how the proposed system improves efficiency and reduces unnecessary inquiries between tenants and boarding house owners.


1.4 Significance of the Project
The Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website is expected to benefit the following groups:

Tenants and Students:
The system provides tenants with easy access to accurate and updated boarding house vacancy information, reducing the need for physical searching and minimizing time wasted on unavailable rooms.

Boarding House Owners:
The platform offers a convenient way to manage and update room availability, reducing repeated inquiries and improving communication with potential tenants.
Institution and Community:
The study contributes to improving housing accessibility within the community by promoting an organized and efficient method of managing boarding house vacancies.
Future Researchers and Developers:
This study may serve as a reference for future research related to accommodation management systems, vacancy tracking platforms, and web-based information systems.
1.5 Scope and Limitations 
Scope of the Study
This study focuses on the design and development of a web-based Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website intended for use by boarding house owners and tenants, particularly students seeking affordable accommodations. The system is designed to allow boarding house owners to update room availability and enable tenants to view available boarding house rooms online.
The study covers the analysis of current vacancy tracking practices, system design, development, and basic evaluation of the proposed system’s effectiveness in improving information accessibility and efficiency.

Limitations of the Study
The study is limited to tracking boarding house room availability only. Features such as online payment processing, rental agreements, tenant background verification, and reservation or booking functionalities are excluded. The system’s effectiveness depends on the accuracy and consistency of updates provided by boarding house owners. Additionally, the study does not cover mobile application development, is limited to a web-based platform, and is strictly restricted to boarding houses located within Cagayan de Oro City. 


1.6 Definition of Terms
For clarity, the following terms are defined as used in this study:
Boarding House – A residential establishment that provides rooms for rent, typically on a long-term basis.
Tenant – An individual seeking or renting a room in a boarding house.
Boarding House Owner – A person responsible for managing and renting out rooms in a boarding house.
Vacancy – The availability of a room for rent within a boarding house.
Vacancy Tracking – The process of monitoring and updating the availability status of boarding house rooms.
Web-Based System – A system that operates through a web browser and is accessible via the internet.
Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website – The proposed web-based platform developed to manage and display real-time boarding house vacancy information.

3.1 Requirements Gathering
During the requirements gathering phase of the VacanSee System, a document review was conducted to collect essential information that served as the basis for the system design and development. The documents reviewed included boarding house inquiry records, availability listings, student accommodation search notes, and informal records related to housing inquiries. 


These materials provided important insights into the difficulties students experience when searching for available boarding houses and how information about vacancies is currently managed. The existing situation was observed in chronological order from start to end. The process begins when a student looks for a boarding house by asking around, visiting nearby areas, or checking informal listings. Students often rely on word-of-mouth recommendations or social media posts to find available rooms. If a boarding house is found, the student personally inquires about availability, rent, and rules. If no vacancy is available, the student moves to another location and repeats the process. Once a boarding house is found with an available slot, the student negotiates with the owner and completes the rental arrangement. Overall, the process is manual, time-consuming, and often requires physically visiting multiple locations before securing a place to stay. An interview was conducted at the University of Science and Technology of Southern Philippines (USTP). The respondents were students who have experienced difficulty in finding available boarding houses near the university. They were selected because they directly face challenges related to accommodation and vacancy searching. The interview guide included questions about their experiences in finding boarding houses, difficulties encountered in locating available rooms, and suggestions for improving access to vacancy information. Prior to the interview, consent was obtained from the respondents. During the interview, notes were taken and key responses were recorded. After the interview, the information gathered was reviewed and organized to ensure accuracy and completeness. One of the main challenges identified is the difficulty students face in finding available boarding houses, especially during peak enrollment periods. Since vacancy information is not centralized, students often spend a lot of time walking around or asking multiple landlords before finding an available room. Another issue is the lack of real-time updates on room availability, which leads to wasted time and repeated inquiries. Additionally, some boarding houses are not publicly advertised, making it harder for students to know their options. Overall, the current process is inefficient and inconvenient for students. The VacanSee System aims to address these problems by providing a centralized and accessible platform where boarding house vacancies can be easily viewed and updated.

3.2.1 User Definition
After evaluating the system requirements gathered from the respondents, two primary users of the system have been identified. These users are comprised of the Tenant and the Boarding House Owner.


Tenant – The tenant is responsible for searching and selecting suitable boarding houses based on their preferences and needs. They are the primary end-users of the system who interact directly with the platform to explore available boarding units. Tenants can register and log in to the system, search for boarding houses using filters such as location, price, and availability, and submit booking requests. They can also provide feedback and ratings based on their experience, which helps improve the reliability of the system and assists other users in making informed decisions.
Boarding House Owner – The boarding house owner is responsible for managing and maintaining boarding house listings within the system. They provide essential information such as room availability, pricing, amenities, and location details. Owners can register and log in to the system, add and update boarding house information, and monitor booking requests submitted by tenants. They are also responsible for approving or rejecting booking requests, ensuring that the availability status of each unit is accurate and up to date.

3.3 Design
This section presents the design of the system. The proponents worked on the design to meet the specifications described in the system requirements. 3.3.1 Context Level Diagram



Figure 2: Context Level Diagram
The context level diagram (Level 0 Data Flow Diagram) of the proposed Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website shows the system as one main process that interacts with two external entities: the Owner and the Tenant. The diagram focuses only on how data enters and leaves the system. First, the Owner sends boarding unit details and availability updates to the system. This includes information such as the type of unit, rental price, location, and whether it is vacant or occupied. The system stores this information and makes it available online. Next, the Tenant uses the system by sending a search request to view available boarding units. The system then provides a list of vacant units along with their details. If the Tenant chooses a boarding unit, a booking request is sent to the system. The system forwards the booking information to the Owner for confirmation. After the Owner approves the request, the system sends a booking confirmation to the Tenant. Overall, the system acts as a bridge between the Owner and the Tenant by organizing vacancy information and making the renting process easier and more efficient.



3.3.2 Data Flow Diagram















Figure 4: Data Flow Diagram










The Level 1 Data Flow Diagram of the Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website shows the internal processes of the system and how data moves between the external entities, processes, and data stores. The system involves two external entities: the Owner and the Tenant. The process begins when either the Owner or the Tenant submits their login information to the User Authentication process. The system verifies the login credentials and sends a login status response. Once successfully authenticated, the Owner can access the Manage Boarding House Information process. In this process, the Owner submits boarding unit details, uploads pictures, and updates availability. The system then stores or updates this information in the Boarding House Database. After logging in, the Tenant can use the Search and Filter Boarding Unit process. The Tenant sends a search request and may include a budget range to filter available boarding units. The system retrieves the relevant data from the Boarding House Database and sends the vacancy list and boarding unit details back to the Tenant. If the Tenant decides to book a boarding unit, a booking request is sent to the Booking and Approval process. The system records the booking information in the Booking Records data store and sends a booking notification to the Owner. The Owner reviews the request and sends an approval or rejection response back to the system. If approved, the system sends a booking confirmation to the Tenant. After a successful booking transaction, the Tenant can submit a star rating through the Manage Ratings process. The system then stores the rating information in the Ratings Database. Overall, the Level 1 Data Flow Diagram shows how the system manages authentication, boarding unit information, search and filtering, booking transactions, and ratings in an organized and structured manner, ensuring secure and efficient interaction between the Owner and the Tenant.

The process begins with authentication, where owners submit their log-in details. If the credentials are false, they are looped back to the log-in screen; if true, they access the primary system menu. From the system menu, owners can navigate three independent operational streams. The first stream allows owners to manage vacancies, enabling a cascading sequence where they upload photos, notify the tenant, and proceed to process bookings by choosing to approve or reject them. The second stream allows direct modification of active records, where owners can update room status between 

available and occupied, edit house details like price or photos, view pending bookings, and route back into the booking approval/rejection process. The third stream handles data maintenance; when an owner chooses to generate an owner report, the system initiates a parallel processing fork. One branch backs up data directly to the database, while the parallel branch uses a decision diamond to check if the oldest file is greater than one month. If true, the file is downloaded to the PC and deleted from the database; if the file is less than one month, it bypasses deletion and joins the database backup line at the synchronization bar. The workflow concludes when the owner selects logout from the system menu, terminating the session at the final state node.

The process begins with secure administrator authentication, where inputting credentials leads to a validation choice node that loops back on failure and grants access to the central system menu on success. From the system menu, the administrator can pivot into three specialized oversight branches. The first branch enables user moderation, allowing the admin to access all user accounts, manage system settings, and oversee the flag system to handle reported accounts. The second branch allows the administrator to view all system activity, providing direct sub-menus to moderate user reviews or oversee the platform-wide flag system. The third branch governs data auditing, where the admin can generate system-wide reports. This action triggers a parallel execution fork: one line instantly backs up active records to the database, while the concurrent line checks if the oldest file is over one month old. If the file is older than a month, it is downloaded to a local PC and deleted from the database before both paths merge at a synchronization bar. This synchronization bar feeds into a massive parallel processing block that simultaneously handles backing up the global database, archiving old records, syncing data across CDO, monitoring system logs, and updating CDO region data before finally exiting the workflow at the session termination node.

3.5 Test & Evaluation

The Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website (VacanSee) will undergo a structured testing and evaluation process to ensure its functionality, performance, security, and usability align with the needs of both tenants and boarding house owners. Through a combination of  black-box testing, white-box testing, performance testing, and usability testing, the system will be assessed for reliability and efficiency before full-scale deployment within the university community. All testing results will be comprehensively documented, and any identified issues will be systematically addressed to enhance overall system reliability and platform efficiency. The findings from these technical and user evaluations will be directly utilized to refine the web architecture, ensuring that the VacanSee System is fully optimized, secure, and capable of delivering real-time vacancy tracking updates before its official launch.

3.5.1 Black Box Testing
Black-box testing will be conducted to evaluate the functionality of the Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website (VacanSee) without analyzing its internal code structure. This approach ensures that the platform meets its functional requirements from an end-user perspective. Several black-box testing techniques will be applied to validate system behavior, including:
Input Classification Testing – Input fields such as login credentials, housing search filters, and account registration forms will be tested using both valid and invalid inputs to verify that the system correctly processes different types of data. For example, invalid email formats and empty password fields will be tested during tenant and owner registration and authentication.
Limit Condition Testing – Room price inputs, room capacity numbers, character limits for boarding house descriptions, and file size restrictions for uploaded property images will be tested at their minimum, maximum, and beyond acceptable values to ensure proper validation and error handling.
Rule-Based Scenario Testing – Different user roles, specifically Tenants/Students, Boarding House Owners, and Administrators, will be tested to confirm that each role has appropriate access to platform functionalities. This will ensure that permissions, such as the ability to update room vacancy status, modify property listings, and view administrative dashboards, are correctly enforced.
User Error Simulation – Test cases will replicate common user mistakes, such as leaving required fields blank when adding a room listing, incorrect login attempts, and unauthorized actions like trying to update a boarding house listing owned by another user. This will help evaluate how well the system handles errors and prevents data integrity risks.

3.5.2 White Box Testing
White-box testing will be conducted to evaluate the internal logic, structure, and code implementation of the Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website (VacanSee). This approach ensures that the system’s core functionalities are optimized, secure, and free from logical errors. Several white-box testing techniques will be employed, including:
Control Structure Testing – The platform’s decision-making logic, such as the authentication process, role-based access control, and vacancy status changes, will be analyzed to ensure all internal execution paths function correctly and securely. This confirms that conditional checks safely separate Tenant/Student views from the administrative tools of Boarding House Owners.
Data Flow Analysis – The movement of data within the system, particularly during user account creation, housing preferences filtering, and booking inquiries management, will be examined to detect issues such as uninitialized variables, data type mismatches, or security vulnerabilities in database query strings.
Path Coverage Testing – All possible execution paths in the web platform's code, including security conditional branches (such as successful dashboard routing vs. unauthorized route access attempts within ProtectedRoute), will be tested to confirm that no sequence of source code operations leads to system failure or layout breakage.
Iteration Testing – Repetitive and looping processes, such as displaying paginated boarding house search results and mapping real-time listings by geographical filters, will be evaluated under varying conditions, including edge cases, to ensure they pull and display information correctly and efficiently.
Code Optimization and Maintainability – The underlying structure of the codebase (built with TypeScript and ReactJS) will be analyzed using specialized automated test suites for performance efficiency, logical readability, and architectural scalability. Best practices, such as modular component structure, proper state management, and clear code comments, will be evaluated to facilitate future feature upgrades and seamless system maintenance.
3.5.3 Performance Testing
To ensure the Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website (VacanSee) operates efficiently under various conditions, performance testing will be conducted. This testing will evaluate the platform’s responsiveness, stability, and scalability when handling multiple concurrent users and large data transactions. The goal is to ensure the system performs optimally without degradation in user experience. Several performance testing techniques will be utilized, including:
Capacity Testing – The platform will be evaluated under different levels of user activity to measure its response time, database efficiency, and overall system performance. This ensures the system can handle expected and peak usage scenarios—such as students searching for accommodations during peak enrollment periods—without affecting usability.
Resilience Testing – The system will be subjected to high operational conditions, such as a high number of simultaneous logins, bulk vacancy status updates by multiple owners, and large image file uploads for property listings, to identify potential failure points. This test ensures that the VacanSee platform can recover from high-demand situations without critical system crashes or data loss.
Scalability Testing – The ability of the system to scale as user demand increases will be analyzed. This will include evaluating server resource allocation and database performance to ensure that the platform remains responsive as the user base of students and boarding house owners across Cagayan de Oro City grows.
Transaction Throughput Testing – The number of successful transactions the system can process per second will be measured. This test will focus on key operations such as real-time room availability status changes, tenant inquiries submission, and search filter executions to verify that the platform maintains efficiency in handling multiple simultaneous interactions.
Response Time Testing – The system’s speed in processing user requests, including login authentication, loading matching boarding house listings, and retrieving updated vacancy data, will be evaluated. The objective is to ensure minimal latency and an optimal user experience, even under heavy usage.
Database Performance Testing – The efficiency of relational database queries, indexing, and live availability data retrieval processes will be assessed. This will help prevent slowdowns during advanced property filtering and location-based sorting, ensuring a seamless tracking and browsing experience.

3.5.4 Usability Testing
Usability testing will be conducted to evaluate the Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website (VacanSee) platform’s ease of use, accessibility, and overall user experience. This testing ensures that students and boarding house owners can efficiently track, search, and update room vacancies without confusion. Several usability testing techniques will be utilized, including:
Navigation Testing – The web platform’s layout, dashboard structures, and advanced search filters will be tested to ensure users can easily navigate between pages, browse room listings, and locate specific accommodation options based on location or budget without difficulty.
Task Efficiency Testing – Test participants will perform key user actions such as a student executing advanced room filtering and submitting a booking inquiry, or an owner updating a room's vacancy status from "Available" to "Occupied". The time taken and any interface frictions encountered will be recorded to streamline the application workflow.
Mobile Responsiveness Testing – The system operates on a web browser, so it will be tested across various desktop, tablet, and mobile devices. This ensures that the interface, tables, and dashboards dynamically adapt to different screen sizes, providing a seamless user experience for students searching for rooms on the go.
Accessibility and Readability Testing – The system's text layouts, interactive status badges, color-coded vacancy indicators, and input fields will be evaluated to ensure clear visibility and ease of understanding for both tech-savvy students and boarding house owners who may be less familiar with digital tools.
User Feedback Collection – Using standard measurement frameworks like the System Usability Scale (SUS) outlined in Appendix I, test participants will provide quantitative and qualitative feedback on their overall experience. This data will be analyzed to refine UI components and eliminate operational bottlenecks before the official launch.

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
5.1 Conclusions
Based on the results from black box testing, white box testing, performance evaluation, and usability testing, the Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website effectively meets its functional, technical, and usability requirements. The black box testing confirmed that all core user operations such as authentication, real-time vacancy updates, and advanced room searching responded correctly under valid and invalid inputs. White box testing further validated the reliability and maintainability of the source code, with high code coverage and low cyclomatic complexity across critical system paths. Performance testing demonstrated the system’s stability under typical load conditions, proving it is highly stable, handles traffic efficiently, and maintains acceptable CPU and memory usage levels. Usability testing yielded an average System Usability Scale (SUS) score of 83.0, indicating an “Excellent” rating from both student tenants and boarding house owners. These findings affirm that the Boarding House Vacant Tracker Website is a secure, accessible, and user-centered platform suitable for deployment within the community. 
5.2 Recommendations
Integration of Advanced Modules (Overcoming Scope Limitations): Future developers should consider expanding the platform's features by integrating an online booking and reservation system, secure online payment gateways, automated digital rental agreements, and basic tenant background verification. Adding these features would transform the tracker from a simple information directory into a complete, end-to-end property management system.
Mobile Application Development: To further improve accessibility for students and real-time updates for boarding house owners, it is highly recommended to develop a cross-platform mobile application (iOS and Android). This mobile version could feature push notifications to immediately alert tenants when a room matches their search preferences or budget becomes vacant.
Automated Verification and Reminder Systems: Since the system’s effectiveness heavily relies on manual updates from boarding house owners, future versions should implement automated SMS or email reminders prompting owners to confirm or update their room availability status weekly. Additionally, a crowdsourced "report as inaccurate" button could be introduced for tenants to help maintain data integrity.
Geographic Expansion: Future researchers can scale the platform beyond Cagayan de Oro City to encompass neighboring provinces or implement a nationwide mapping system. Integrating Global Positioning System (GPS) technology or interactive map APIs (like Google Maps) would also allow students to visually locate boarding houses relative to their university campus.
Policy Support and Local Integration: It is recommended that the platform be introduced to local student councils or Barangay local government units (LGUs) in Cagayan de Oro. Partnering with them can help build a verified database of legitimate, accredited boarding houses, making the platform safer and more official for incoming students.
Implementing these recommendations will help ensure the platform remains reliable, user-friendly, and well-adapted to the needs of Owners and Tenants.
